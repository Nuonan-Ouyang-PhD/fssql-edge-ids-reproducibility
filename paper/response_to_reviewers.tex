\begin{authorresponsebox}
Dear Editor-in-Chief, Associate Editor, and Reviewers,

Thank you for the detailed review. We have revised the empirical evaluation, added the requested deterministic controller, and narrowed the claims where the new measurements do not support the earlier interpretation. The submitted FSSQL-R formulation and nominal 40-ms/$82^\circ$C caps are retained, but the revised empirical tables are replacement evidence, not a numerical reproduction of the earlier replay tables.

Two corrections require explicit explanation. First, the original execution artifacts were unavailable. We reconstructed and froze the detector, preprocessing, calibration, and scheduler implementations; they are not claimed to be byte-identical historical artifacts. Second, review of the initial Pi 4B online implementation exposed differences between the declared and executed batch size, risk/controller logic, masking, reward, and background load, together with timing and power-alignment problems. We exclude that campaign and use only the corrected R6 Pi 4B campaign in this submission. Earlier files remain archived as superseded material.

The submitted paper stated that test-set TPR/FPR supplied the reward-quality terms. As described, this permits test information to affect the controller objective. Because the historical artifacts are unavailable, we do not reinterpret it as a verified wording error. The revised reward uses validation-partition quality terms only.

The official detector test was opened on 11 September 2026. The October implementation corrections and Q-policy reconstruction occurred afterwards on development data without reopening the test. The detector, preprocessor, threshold, and validation-quality hashes remain unchanged. We have removed the earlier claim that the test was opened after all of the now-reported hardware work. The saved test aggregates and their provenance limits are reported separately from scheduler-development results.
\end{authorresponsebox}

\section*{Associate Editor: contribution and learning value}
\commentbox{The contribution should be separated from prior shielding/RL work and simpler controllers. Clarify the relation to earlier energy-aware and multi-objective schedulers, define Unshielded-Q, discuss recent shielded-RL IDS work, and include a conventional threshold controller.}
\responsebox{The revised Related Work states that shielding and constrained RL are established ideas. It compares the action semantics and measurement boundary with recent TSUSC sustainable scheduling, TMC edge inference, TIFS IDS, and the suggested 2026 self-healing IDS study (DOI 10.32604/cmc.2026.074274). We do not claim novelty for shielding alone.

Unshielded-Q is explicitly the reconstructed FSSQL state/reward/Q implementation without masking, evaluated in the Pi 3B+ campaign. It is not described as an exact executable reproduction of an earlier author scheduler. The unavailable predecessor execution assets prevent that stronger equivalence claim; the paper confines its contribution to the defined revision implementation and measured comparisons.

The corrected Pi 4B Threshold baseline reads temperature, queue load, and risk, with hysteresis and fixed light/heavy roles. Its temperature/queue branches are tested as implementation behavior but are not activated under the evaluated cold, zero-backlog conditions. It is a no-training, unshielded comparator, not a risk-only branch renamed as the requested controller.}
\changebox{Main Sections II, IV and V-C; Table I; Supplement S4 and S7.}
\commentbox{The incremental value of learning over the shield is unclear. Report dispersion, paired tests, learning schedules, state-space size, and whether any improvement justifies the complexity.}
\responsebox{The corrected evidence does not establish an incremental practical advantage for Q-learning. On Pi 3B+, Safe-Greedy has higher F1/reward and lower mean latency and switching cost. On Pi 4B, B0/B1 F1 is close (FSSQL-R about 0.9598; Safe-Greedy 0.9608), but B2 FSSQL-R falls to 0.8869 with FPR 0.5199. The B2 mean paired F1 difference is $-0.0739$; the exact two-sided sign-flip $p$ is 0.0625 at five pairs. All five seeds are retained.

The comparison uses the same guard and complete one-step reward, including security, energy, backlog, and switching terms. Safe-Greedy maximizes that immediate reward; FSSQL-R selects by its learned Q values. The evidence therefore evaluates the incremental value of learned selection under the sampled workloads, subject to the model-based training and state-support limitations. Checkpoint stability is reported separately from a mathematical convergence claim.}
\changebox{Main IV-B, V-E, VI-B--C and VII-B; Tables IV--VI; Supplement S3--S4.}

\section*{Associate Editor: deployment, safety, and scope}
\commentbox{The setup should demonstrate real deployment cost: distinguish measured and reconstructed quantities, provide an online Pi run, measure controller/tail and switching overhead, and validate physical energy.}
\responsebox{The corrected Pi 4B campaign comprises 60 accepted runs, 36,000 windows, and 576,000 record evaluations. Every window actually processes 16 inputs, executes RiskProxy, constructs the state and guard, selects an action, and runs the selected resident detector. Labels are loaded after the decision loop for scoring; the reward is label-free. These online measurements are distinct from the model-based environment used for Q training.

We define the boundaries rather than call every recorded interval complete program cost. Controller time is measured after preprocessing through action binding; E2E spans preprocessing through post-action telemetry. Reward calculation, serialization, and end-of-run scoring are outside E2E. The resident-model switching timer measures binding rather than model reload. Mac-side KM003C sampling is outside the Pi decision path.

Physical energy is integrated over the measured Mac ACK interval and includes idle periods, background work, and tail scoring/writeout. The raw traces cover both integration boundaries, with no endpoint extrapolation. Energy differences change sign across conditions and do not support consistent savings.}
\changebox{Main V-D and VI-D; Fig. 2; Supplement S5.}
\commentbox{Report calibration sizes, residual distributions and empirical coverage; test a second configuration or changed background load; scope safety and MCU claims; correct presentation inconsistencies.}
\responsebox{Each device has its own fit, margin, and held-out coverage splits: 2880 windows per split over 72 cells. Pi 3B+ joint coverage is 2879/2880; Pi 4B is 2877/2880. All misses are retained. Residual ECDFs and the actual residual margins plus buffers are supplied. The conditional admissibility statement is not presented as an unconditional complete-pipeline guarantee.

Pi 4B adds a second configuration and real B1/B2 load-process execution. PID, affinity, duty configuration and liveness are recorded; an independent CPU-utilization curve is not available, so the paper does not claim exactly measured 50\%/100\% utilization. The thermal cap remains nonbinding. Claims are limited to the tested Pi configurations and data. Revised tables/figures replace the inconsistent earlier aggregates, and the parameter and timing definitions are consolidated.}
\changebox{Main V-B--D, VI-A, VIII; Tables II--III; Supplement S2, S4--S5.}

\section*{Reviewer 1, Comment 1: practical advantage and uncertainty}
\commentbox{Justify Q-learning over Safe-Greedy. The ten-seed means lack dispersion, confidence intervals and baseline rows; small violation-count means may be driven by one or two runs. Report paired-seed inference and discuss complexity and switching in absolute terms.}
\responsebox{We replaced the old comparison with explicit baseline rows, run-level summaries, paired differences, and seed-level tables. The Pi 3B+ matched comparison gives FSSQL-R/Safe-Greedy mean F1 0.8523/0.8708, mean reward 0.5434/0.5839, and mean E2E 8.3149/7.9775 ms. Switching cost is 5.348/0 per run. Exact sign-flip $p$ is 0.00195 for F1, reward, and switching cost, and 0.00586 for E2E; both methods have zero observed violations. We therefore do not retain the submitted interpretation of a learning-induced safety gain.

Corrected Pi 4B data are analyzed independently. The online table includes all four policies, FPR, reward, latency, controller time, and measured energy. Safe-Greedy is no longer an all-positive reference: its FPR is 0.1327 and mean F1 0.9608 in all three conditions. Threshold is close at F1 0.9605/FPR 0.1341; Static-Light retains the poor FISVDD operating point. The old Pi 4B positive F1 differences and seed-5102 account belonged to the excluded worker and have been removed.

R6 FSSQL-R has 22.6, 23.0, and 162.8 mean switches/run under B0, B1 and B2, respectively. Every switch incurs the fixed $0.02$ reward penalty. This is not equated with the measured resident binding time or with physical joules. The corrected data show additional switching without an established detection or energy benefit.

Five Pi 4B pairs limit exact inference. We report the exact sign-flip test and paired percentile interval used by the R6 analysis, not the earlier excluded campaign's tests. For B2 the F1 difference has interval $[-0.0888,-0.0568]$, but exact $p=0.0625$; the interval is not used to assert a two-sided $p<0.05$ result. Multiple endpoints remain descriptive.}
\changebox{Main Tables IV--VI, VI-B--D, VII-B; Supplement Tables S2--S4 and the supplied run-level/paired CSV files.}
\begin{excerptbox}\emph{Neither evaluated Pi regime establishes a practical need for the learned layer over the simpler same-guard rule. B0/B1 Pi 4B detection scores are close, while B2 and the Pi 3B+ binding comparison favor Safe-Greedy.}\end{excerptbox}

\section*{Reviewer 2, Comment 1: novelty and related work}
\commentbox{Shielding and constrained RL are established. Explain the relation to earlier author schedulers and whether Unshielded-Q is the earlier method; otherwise include that method. Discuss the recent safety-aware self-healing IDS study and clarify the technical distinction.}
\responsebox{We agree that feasibility shielding alone is not a new RL technique. The paper now separates the established ingredients from the deployment question being evaluated. Our actions select resident detectors; the guard uses device-calibrated latency and next-temperature estimates; the evaluation measures the controller path and DC input energy. The contribution is limited to that implementation and its observed trade-offs, including results unfavorable to the learned controller.

The suggested study, ``Safety-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Self-Healing Intrusion Detection in 5G-Enabled IoT Networks,'' is cited as Malwi et al. (2026), DOI 10.32604/cmc.2026.074274. Table I also compares the control decisions and measurement boundaries of recent TSUSC, TMC and TIFS work supplied in the revised bibliography.

Unshielded-Q is now defined as the reconstructed Q policy with the same state/reward formulation and without the admissible-action mask. The unavailable historical implementation prevents that stronger equivalence from being verified, and we do not invent a predecessor baseline from its reported averages. The revised empirical claim is therefore a comparison with the specified current baselines, not proof of superiority over every predecessor.

We also clarify that Safe-Greedy shares the shield and complete one-step reward with FSSQL-R. It includes the same security, energy, backlog, and switching terms, choosing the admissible action with the greatest immediate reward. FSSQL-R instead uses learned Q values. Model-based training, sparse state support, and the stationary zero-backlog workload limit the interpretation of this comparison.}

\begin{excerptbox}\emph{It is a same-guard, same-reward myopic baseline; FSSQL-R instead selects the admissible action with the largest learned Q value.}\end{excerptbox}
